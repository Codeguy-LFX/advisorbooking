How to Define Your Ideal Client as a Financial Advisor

Your next 20 clients look like your top 20. Here is how to work out who they are and how to get in front of more of them.

NDNick De JesusNovember 1, 2026

Key takeaways

  • Pull the 20% of clients you value most. That list is your ideal client profile in raw form.
  • Look for shared traits: occupation, how you met, location, communication habits, interests, news sources.
  • Keep asking why until you can describe your best clients in one clear sentence.
  • Build a list of people who match and reach them where they already spend their time.
  • A message written for their specific situation beats a generic retirement planning pitch.

The fastest way to define your ideal client as a financial advisor is to pull your top 20% of clients, write down what they have in common, and then go find more people who match that description.

This post is part of a series. The previous post: “How to ask for referrals without sounding like you are asking.”

Asking for referrals the right way works. But a 2026 financial advisor marketing study found that the fastest-growing RIAs rely the least on referrals. So what do they do instead?

Here is the pattern: firms that get most of their new clients from one clearly defined niche show up more often among the fastest-growing practices. They know exactly who they serve, and they go find more of those people.

Here is how to figure out who that is for your firm, and how to get in front of more of them.

1. Pull your top 20% of clients

What: if you have 100 clients, find the 20 you value most.

Look for: highest revenue, easiest to work with, most likely to refer, longest relationship.

Goal: a short list of the clients you would clone if you could.

2. Find what they have in common

Ask yourself:

  • What do they do for a living? Business owners, physicians, executives?
  • How did you get them? Referral, CPA, event, LinkedIn, email, cold call, SMS, an ad?
  • Where do they live and work?
  • How do they like to communicate, and which platforms do they actually use?
  • What hobbies or interests do they share?
  • How do they consume news and information?

Keep asking “why” until the similarities are obvious. A pattern in the similarities is your ideal client profile (ICP).

Pro tip: the more time you spend on these questions, the more clarity you will get on who your ideal client really is.

3. Go find more of them

When: once you can describe your top 20 clients clearly in one sentence.

Examples: “Business owners in South Florida doing $5M to $20M who are 3 to 5 years from selling.” Or “Physicians in Dallas within 10 years of retirement.”

Then: build a list of people who match, and reach out where they already spend their time most. A message that is specifically personalized to their situation always beats a generic “we help families plan for retirement.”

Example language:

“Hi Dr. Patel, we work with a number of physicians in the Dallas area who are within 10 years of retiring and want a clear plan for the transition. Open to a quick conversation to see if it’d be useful?”

Why this works

You choose who you talk to. Your effort goes only to people who look like your best 20%, and they tend to close faster, stay longer and be worth more. Same effort. Better book of business.

Common questions

What is an ideal client profile for a financial advisor?

An ideal client profile (ICP) is a one-sentence description of the clients you would clone if you could. You build it by pulling your top 20% of clients and writing down what they have in common: occupation, how you met them, where they live and work, how they communicate, and what interests they share.

How do I find my ideal client as an RIA?

Start with the 20% of clients you value most, based on revenue, ease of working together, likelihood to refer, and length of relationship. Then look for shared traits and keep asking why until the pattern is obvious. Once you can describe them in one sentence, build a list of people who match.

Should financial advisors focus on a niche?

Firms that get most of their new clients from one clearly defined niche show up more often among the fastest-growing practices. A niche lets you choose who you talk to and write messages specific to their situation, which beats a generic retirement planning pitch.

This is general information for advisory firms, not legal, compliance or investment advice.
