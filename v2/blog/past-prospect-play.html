Reactivation

The past-prospect play: your fastest first meetings are already in your CRM

Before you buy a single new list, work the people who already know your name.

NDNick De JesusNovember 3, 2026

Every advisory firm has a second pipeline it never works. It’s sitting in the CRM.

It’s the business owner who took a first meeting two years ago and said “not yet.” The couple who asked for a proposal and went quiet. The referral who never booked. The people from last spring’s client event.

They already know your name. Some of them have already met an advisor at your firm. That makes them the fastest source of first meetings you have. It’s also why we start here, in week 1, before any new outreach goes out.

Who counts as a past prospect

  • Anyone who took a first meeting and didn’t become a client
  • Anyone who asked for a proposal or a second opinion and went quiet
  • Referrals and introductions that never booked
  • Event, seminar and webinar attendees
  • Old leads from any source, if they fit the clients you serve best

Leave out current clients, anyone who asked not to be contacted, and anyone your compliance team has flagged.

How to run it

  1. Clean the list first. Remove duplicates, bounced emails and people who don’t fit. Fill in missing names and companies. A smaller clean list beats a big messy one.
  2. Group people by why they stalled. “The timing wasn’t right” is a different conversation from “we chose another advisor.”
  3. Send from the advisor’s own mailbox. These people met a person, not a brand. A short, plain email from that person gets read.
  4. Ask one easy question. Not a pitch. Something like: “Is planning for the sale of the business still on your list this year?”
  5. Get your CCO’s approval before anything sends. Every message and every follow-up.
  6. Follow up twice, then stop. Short and polite, a few days apart.
  7. Answer every reply within 1 business day. A reply that waits a week goes cold.
  8. Book the ones who fit. Check each reply against what you count as a qualified meeting. Say a polite no to the rest.

What usually goes wrong

  • Sending the newsletter and calling it reactivation. A newsletter keeps you in touch. It rarely starts a conversation.
  • Long emails. If it scrolls, it’s too long.
  • Nobody owns the replies. The play works only if someone reads and answers every reply, fast.
  • Skipping compliance. It costs more time later than it saves now.

Why it comes first

New outreach takes time to set up. Domains have to warm up and messages have to be approved. Past prospects need none of that. They’re the quickest way to put real conversations on an advisor’s calendar while the rest of the system gets ready.

This is general information, not compliance advice. Your CCO has the final word on what you send.
